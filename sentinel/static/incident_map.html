How to read this console

Dispatch link
The tower's connection to the outside world (radio/satellite/cell). When it is down, the tower keeps working: it still detects, classifies and logs. ALERTs wait in an outbox on the device and go out when the link comes back. The button in the header simulates losing and restoring it.
ALERT / MONITOR / LOG
ALERT: likely wildfire, reported to dispatch. MONITOR: unclear or small, the tower keeps re-checking it. LOG: harmless (campfire, chimney, controlled burn), kept locally. Fog and cloud are ignored.
Direction from camera
A single camera measures the direction to the smoke, not the distance. The pin is placed at an assumed distance along that direction. If you know where the fire is, use Correct location and click the map.
Downwind cone
Where the fire and smoke would drift with the current wind over 1, 3 or 6 hours (10% of wind speed, a grass-fire rule of thumb). A guide for where to look first, not a prediction.
Camera feed
In the field each tower camera is wired to this computer over the tower's local network: no internet in the loop. For the demo we replay real recorded tower footage (HPWREN FIgLib, stored on this device) as that feed, or you upload a photo.
Wind
From the tower's own weather station (emulated here from recorded readings stored on this device). No internet needed.
What needs internet
Nothing for detection, classification, the map, wind, history or Ask Sentinel. Only an ALERT uses the dispatch link: the report (~13 KB) and a weather forecast. Everything else stays on the tower.
Live watch
Replays a tower camera frame by frame. Each batch of frames produces a situation update in the incident's timeline.
Ask Sentinel
A language model running on this device that answers from the local incident log. Nothing is sent to the cloud.
